Origins & Vision

The Genesis of Table

"Food should not be an intellectual construction. It is an emotional communion between soil, artisan, fire, and palate."

Table opened its doors in 2013 on the historic Rue de Prague, adjacent to the bustling Marché d'Aligre. Bruno Verjus sought to challenge the rigid French haute cuisine traditions that often favored complex sauces, theatrical masking, and overly manicured textures over true ingredient vitality.

The physical layout was designed intentionally: no traditional dining tables isolated in quiet corners. Instead, a curving, pewter-topped bar winds through the room. Guests sit shoulder-to-shoulder with one another and face the open kitchen. There is no wall, no service pass, and no secret back room.

Over the following decade, this radical focus on authenticity garnered worldwide acclaim: receiving its first Michelin star in 2018, its second star in 2022, and reaching the No. 3 position on The World's 50 Best Restaurants list in 2024.

Reverence For Living Produce

The Daily Harvest

At Table, products are not conserved in advance or frozen. Whole wild turbots arrive still moist from the Atlantic breeze. Mushrooms carry the morning humidity of the forest.

Cooking times are counted in seconds rather than hours. Fish is poached gently, crustacean shells are rendered in pure nut butters, and wild herbs are added at the precise temperature where their volatile aromatic oils burst without wilting.
